# Known limitations

What this project does not do, or does only in part, as it stands. It is a reference implementation run against a local simulator of the DIAN, with synthetic data; it is not in production use.

## Relationship with the DIAN

- **Not certified.** No CUFE, CUDE or software security code produced here was ever accepted by the DIAN. Every "accepted" comes from the local simulator in `simulator/`.
- **What validates the documents.** The local simulator (the subset of the rules of annex 1.9 in `simulator/src/rules.ts`; ZD05 is a notification there), the official XSDs of the DIAN toolbox, xmlsec1 for the signatures, and, once, the 2019 schematron of the toolbox (`make dian-schematron`: 24 findings from rules older than annex 1.9, each listed with its reason in the Makefile; the run depends on the demo documents).
- **No WS-Security.** The signature of the SOAP header that the real web service requires (section 7.5) is not built. The simulator does not check it, and its ApplicationResponse is unsigned.
- **Test certificates only.** Documents are signed with synthetic self-signed RSA certificates, re-dated by rewriting their DER. Only the simulator accepts them; the DIAN would not.
- **What could not be checked without the real service:** the name and NIT in the RUT (FAJ43b, FAJ44b, RUT01), the registered reception emails (FAJ71, FAK55), the issuer as owner of the certificate (ZE03), the ONAC chain of trust (ZD05, DC38 to DC46), the real answers, delays and failures of the service, and the habilitación test set.
- **Calls that may have arrived.** Whether a call without an answer reached the service is judged from its cURL error code. The next attempt asks GetStatus first, and a document sent twice is answered by rule 90.

## Signature and certificates

- SigningCertificate lists the chain only when the uploaded file brings it, while DC25 asks for three Cert entries; X509Data carries only the signing certificate, as the toolbox example Generica.xml does (10.5.2 asks for the whole chain).
- RSA with SHA-256 only.
- The signing time is the clock of the server, not synchronized with the legal time of the INM (10.11); time synchronization belongs to Infrastructure.
- No revocation check (CRL or OCSP) of the signing certificate.
- PKCS#12 files exported with legacy encryption (RC2, 3DES) do not open under OpenSSL 3 without its legacy provider: the upload answers that the file could not be opened.
- A certificate must be valid at the instant of the signature. One that expires between the issue and the signature stops the chain at the signing step, before anything is sent; the document is retried once a valid certificate is uploaded.

## Calculation and XML

- Withholdings practiced by the customer go in WithholdingTaxTotal as informative values (the annex describes the group for self-withholdings; 11.9.1 keeps them out of the totals), and notes carry none (their XSD has no such group).
- Own software only (ppp 000, no technology provider); only code 999 items in the demo.
- Phones over ten digits are left out; descriptions are split at 300 characters and notes at 500.
- Rates outside table 13.3.11 are a notification; official concept names shorter than the 20 characters of CBF04 and DBF04, as in the 2023 examples; the debit note example of 11.4.5 does not match its own data.
- The optional rounding of IVA to 10 pesos (5.2.1.2) is not applied.
- ReteICA rates are entered as percentages (9.66 per thousand is 0.966 %).
- The CUFE or CUDE, the issue date and time and the due date are final only once the simulator accepts the document: a generation or a signature on a later day moves them (FAD09e). The webhook invoice.issued keeps the first dates.

## Delivery and PDF

- The AttachedDocument is built from the table of the annex (the toolbox has no example); its ID repeats every year, its literals stay those of an invoice for notes, and ValidatorID names the simulator. The PDF is named after the XML.
- dompdf: no OpenType layout, page numbers and watermark drawn by a page script, simplified SVG, fixed margins and page breaks, no PDF/UA or PDF/A. Its warnings are silenced while it draws; the result is checked to be a PDF of at least one page. The PDF is drawn inside the lock of its document.
- Texts are in English until the Polish phase. The 2 MB limit of section 9.1 is checked but never reached by the demo.
- Email is delivered at least once: a worker or a function that dies after the mail server took the email sends it again. SPF, DKIM and DMARC are DNS records of the domain identity of the stage prod, which the owner of the domain publishes: no hosted zone is managed here (Edge, below).

## API and webhooks

- API keys have two levels (read_only, read_write), no scopes per resource and no IP allow list. They are stored as SHA-256, enough for their 238 bits of randomness; there is no pepper.
- Rate limits count in fixed windows of 60 seconds, so a burst of twice the limit fits around the edge of a window. Web sessions are limited on sign-in, sign-up, password recovery, invitation links, company creation, the invoice preview and the search; other session routes have no limit of their own.
- In production webhook endpoints must be https on port 443: the functions open no other port toward the internet, so an endpoint on another port cannot be subscribed (422 when the subscription is created or changed) and a subscription saved before is refused before each attempt.
- Rotating a webhook secret replaces it at once, without a period in which both secrets sign.
- Webhooks are delivered at least once and in no particular order; their bodies are built when the event is published. Answers of an endpoint are read up to 64 KB and the rest is discarded.
- An Idempotency-Key is kept 24 hours. A request with one runs in a single database transaction with its key, so a deadlock inside it is not retried in place: it answers 5xx and the same key can be used again.
- The API reference (Scramble) is public and loads Stoplight Elements 8.4.2 from unpkg.com. Behind the edge it shares the origin of the session of the web app; the browser runs those two files only with the bytes their Subresource Integrity pins, and the page has a Content-Security-Policy of its own that allows its inline scripts by hash (docs/infrastructure.md). The integrity is added to the page of Scramble by a middleware that finds its two tags; a version of Scramble that changes them fails the reference (500) and its test until security-headers.json is updated. Its styles may be inline.
- Actions done with an API key have no user in created_by; their timelines name the key.

## Payments and business rules

- No payment gateway: payments are recorded by hand or through the API. Payments of one company are recorded one at a time (a lock on the company).
- As designed: notes freeze the rates of their invoice; the prefill of a void note matches the invoice; credited and debited amounts count accepted notes only; payment status, overdue and aging follow the rules of the receivables; negative balances cannot be used; a void releases a whole allocation; receipts are not electronic documents; drafts are deleted for good.
- Payments shares the database and the transaction with Billing through its ports, and Workspace reads the tables of other modules; splitting them into services changes both.

## Security

- No second factor (TOTP or WebAuthn).
- No email verification at sign-up, by decision: the account is created and signed in at once, so sign-up still tells a refusal from a success, and a refusal means the email or the NIT is already registered. Mitigation: one message for a taken email and a taken NIT, without naming the field, and ten sign-up attempts an hour per address.
- Passwords: bcrypt, 12 to 128 characters (bcrypt reads the first 72 bytes), no check against lists of breached passwords, which would need an external service.
- No rotation of the encryption key. APP_KEY encrypts the software PINs, the technical keys and the webhook secrets, and locally the key store (certificates and their passwords) too; changing it needs re-encrypting them, and the key lives in the memory of PHP. In the emulated cloud the key store is envelope encryption under a KMS key and APP_KEY comes from Secrets Manager, whose secret has no rotation function: rotating APP_KEY would need one that re-encrypts every value. The secret of APP_KEY holds nothing else: the credentials of the database are in the secrets of its two identities, which the rotation functions of AWS rotate in prod.
- Accepting an invitation for a new address while signed in as someone else signs that person out; the page says so first.
- Development tools without authentication, on 127.0.0.1 only: the local mailbox (it holds every reset and invitation link), the webhook receiver, and in the emulated cloud the AWS emulator, which takes any credentials and keeps every email SES took. The simulator keeps the PINs and technical keys registered with it in plain text in its state file, as the DIAN, which issues them, knows them.
- Local development uses fixed database credentials (dian/dian and root/root, in compose.yaml and backend/.env.example), and the application user owns the schema it migrates. The Redis password and the simulator token are random per checkout. The emulated cloud has two identities of its own with generated passwords (docs/infrastructure.md); its migrations identity holds CREATE USER, which MySQL grants on the whole server, to create the application identity: it could also alter the users of the local stack and of the tests on the shared MySQL of compose, though not root, which holds SYSTEM_USER. The migrations identity of the tests (tests/database.sql) holds the same for the same reason.
- The security headers of the web app come from the edge (docs/infrastructure.md); the development server of the local stack sends none, so make e2e runs without a policy and make cloud-e2e under it. The policy of the web app allows by their hashes a few fixed style elements of libraries (Reka UI, VueUse) and the inline script of index.html: an upgrade that changes one of them shows as a violation in make cloud-e2e, and the hash goes into security-headers.json. The Server header of FrankenPHP is left to the proxy in front.
- CORS accepts any origin without credentials under /api/v1, for API keys, and is not offered for the CSRF cookie; a session works only from the configured stateful origins, the origin of the edge.
- Dependency audits fail on high and critical advisories. Moderate ones in development tools (minimatch through ESLint and @vue/test-utils, and the copy of minimatch bundled in aws-cdk-lib, which runs at synthesis only, when this was written) wait for their fixes upstream, which Dependabot proposes.

## Operation and scale

- The simulator keeps its whole state in one JSON file, written after every change.
- Searches use LIKE without full-text indexes; the activity and attention queries of the dashboard have no indexes of their own; aging is computed in PHP on each request; the dashboard chunk of the web app is large.
- make stress uses one numbering and one company; deadlock retries and lock waits were not measured at scale.
- The job of a worker killed during it waits REDIS_QUEUE_RETRY_AFTER (45 seconds) before another worker takes it.
- Old operational records are pruned once an hour (config/retention.php): locally by the relay, in the functions by the Laravel scheduler of the function scheduler.
- Every service has a healthcheck; there are no metrics or alerts.
- The production target of the FrankenPHP image of the backend copies no code and installs no dependencies: it serves the local stack only. The functions run the image of the target lambda, which carries the application and its production dependencies.
- The development tools (make stress, make chaos and the checks) and the demo seeder refuse to run outside APP_ENV local or testing. One that is interrupted leaves its synthetic company behind until the next make seed.

## Emulated cloud

- The local AWS emulator is Floci 2.5.0. It accepts the resources of the templates and creates each one for real (an unsupported type fails its stack, FLOCI_SERVICES_CLOUDFORMATION_ALLOW_STUB_UNSUPPORTED_RESOURCE_TYPES=false), but evaluates no IAM policy, resource policy or key policy: the least-privilege roles of the functions, the managed policies, the TLS-only policies of the topic, the queues and the buckets, the topic as the only sender to the queues, the repository policy of ECR and the encryption context of the signing key are checked on the synthesized templates only (the tests of the CDK app and cdk-nag). The functions reach the emulator over plain HTTP, which those policies refuse in AWS.
- Accepted but not applied by the emulator: the encryption key of the topic, the server access logs of the buckets, the storage classes and expirations of their lifecycle rules, and the lifecycle policy and the scan on push of the ECR repository. SES keeps every email at /_aws/ses, an endpoint of the emulator that make cloud-check reads and make cloud-demo clears, and delivers none; the sender address is verified at once, and DKIM, SPF and DMARC are not emulated.
- One environment at a time: both use the same ports and share the DIAN simulator and the MySQL server (each with a database of its own); the emulated cloud serves the production build of the web app from its edge instead of the development server, built again by every make cloud-up. make cloud-demo resets the simulator, as make seed does.
- The identities of the database of the stage local get generated passwords in the Secrets Manager of the emulator: make cloud-up creates the migrations identity from its secret as the MySQL root user, as Aurora creates its master user, and the console function creates the application identity. The admin token of the DIAN simulator, random per checkout in backend/.env, goes to the functions of the stage local as an environment variable, so it is in their configuration and in the template of their stack in the emulator; it is a development tool, and prod has none. The functions get the temporary credentials of their roles from the emulator, which takes any.
- The connections of the functions of the emulated cloud to the MySQL of compose are encrypted (TLS 1.3, and the identities are refused without TLS) but the certificate of the server is not verified: the MySQL of compose signs its own, for another name than db. Prod verifies it against the bundle of RDS in the image (DB_SSL_VERIFY_SERVER_CERT), which only a real account can prove.
- The emulator cannot run the rotation functions of AWS (applications of the Serverless Application Repository, published from accounts of AWS), so the secrets of the identities of the stage local have no rotation schedule, acknowledged in cdk-nag. make cloud-check stands in for the rotation with alternating users: the console function, with the migrations identity as the superuser, does its steps (a new password on the user that is not current, created with the privileges of the identity the first time; a test of the new credentials; a new current version of the secret) and writes the secret through the emulator, which evaluates no IAM: the role of the console function has no permission to write a secret, and in AWS the rotation functions do it.
- Floci 2.5.0 does not apply a SecretStringTemplate built with intrinsic functions (it stored the generated password alone): the secret of the application identity of the stage local therefore names no masterarn, which only the rotation function of AWS reads, and prod keeps it.
- The functions run as Lambda functions in containers the emulator starts through the Docker socket, which only the profile cloud mounts. What the emulator creates carries the name of the project and the label dian-einvoice=floci, by which make cloud-down and make up remove it: the containers of the functions are named dian-einvoice-dian-einvoice-<function>-<id> (the prefix, then the namespace of the emulator, then the name of the function); the registry behind its ECR and its web console are named floci-aws-dian-einvoice-ecr and floci-aws-dian-einvoice-console, and the volume of the registry floci-aws-dian-einvoice-ecr-registry-data, because the emulator puts floci-aws- in front of the containers and volumes it starts besides the functions. The emulator keeps at most 8 containers of functions idle in all (2 per function) and removes one after 5 minutes without an invocation; busy ones are not counted. The registry has no memory limit of compose: the emulator starts it.
- AWS runs a function on a read-only file system but /tmp, as a user that owns nothing; the emulator runs it as an unprivileged user (993:990, FLOCI_SERVICES_LAMBDA_DOCKER_FLAGS) on a writable file system, so a write outside /tmp fails for want of permission rather than for a read-only file system. Root owns every file of the image.
- AWS freezes an execution environment as soon as the function answers; the emulator does not, so work left for after the answer would run here and stop half done in AWS. The application leaves none, and a test refuses the calls that would (docs/infrastructure.md).
- Cold starts of the emulator are not those of AWS, which also pulls and caches the image of a new version first: here a cold start of the API takes about 1 s and a warm answer about 15 ms, which make cloud-check reports as figures of the emulator. Before it starts a container, the emulator checks that the user of Lambda may enter each directory of the working directory by copying it out of the container through the archive API of Docker; for /var/task, the directory of the application (about 125 MB), that took 8 to 9 s, so the image works from / (nothing depends on its working directory).
- Floci 2.5.0 gives back a record a function reported as failed after the visibility timeout of its queue (60 s), whatever visibility the function set; AWS keeps the one set. Here, then, an unreadable message, due again a second later, reaches the dead-letter queue after its 10 receives in about 10 minutes instead of seconds; a record returned before the deadline of its invocation comes back after a minute instead of at once; and a retry due in 2 to 144 s (DIAN_RETRY_DELAYS and its jitter) runs one to three minutes later, a receive spent per minute. make cloud-check therefore stops at the second receive of its unreadable message and purges the chain queue. The emulator fixed this on its main branch on 2026-11-19 (pull request #9003 of floci-io/floci), after 2.5.0 and in no release yet; make cloud-check reads the version of the emulator and, on a later one, waits for the dead-letter queue again.
- The emulator runs one invocation at a time per mapping of a queue: MaximumConcurrency is kept, not applied. The chain of make cloud-check therefore runs one batch at a time, and the time from issue to delivery it reports is that of one consumer; reserved concurrency is applied.
- Behind API Gateway and Lambda, a request carries at most 6 MB (the limit of a synchronous invocation; API Gateway takes 10) and so does an answer, in base64 for binary ones: the largest file the application serves, a zip of at most 2 MB (section 9.1 of the annex), fits; uploads of larger files would need presigned URLs of S3, which the application does not use.
- The emulator serves API Gateway and CloudFront by host name only (<id>.execute-api.localhost.floci.io, <id>.cloudfront.localhost.floci.io), so the ports of the web app and of the API (8601 and 8600) belong to a minimal reverse proxy in front of the distribution (compose service cloud-gateway, nginx), which make cloud-up points at the distribution it deployed (infra/functions/.env.cloud). In AWS there is no such proxy.
- The queues of the stage local hide a message for 60 s, the timeout of their consumers and the least Lambda takes, while the emulator gives failed records back after the visibility timeout of their queue (docs/infrastructure.md); those of prod for 360 s, six times it, as AWS recommends against duplicates after a throttle. A message a killed function held comes back after that time; in the emulator, when the invocation fails outright instead of returning an error, after the 120 s it hides every message it hands a function (the timeout of the function, the 30 s of FLOCI_SERVICES_LAMBDA_INIT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS and 30 s more).
- A message scheduled for later (a transmission or webhook retry) spends one receive of its queue while it waits, invisible: after such a wait its job has up to 9 attempts left of the 10 of maxReceiveCount. A message SNS cannot deliver ends in the same dead-letter queue. A record a consumer could not finish before the deadline of its invocation goes back to the queue at once and also spends a receive.
- One job per event type and queue: the consumer function sends to the dead-letter queue an event whose queue has no job for its type, or more than one; a backend test checks the subscriptions against the routes the CDK app filters on.
- The secrets are read once per execution environment (the configuration is cached when it starts, with them): a rotated secret reaches the environments started after the rotation; those already running keep the old values until Lambda replaces them. For the database this is allowed for twice: the application identity rotates with alternating users, so the credentials of a warm function stay valid until the rotation after next, and the connector reads the secret of its identity again, once, when the database refuses them. The single-user rotation of the migrations identity, used by the console function only, leaves a window of seconds between the change of the password and that of the secret in which a connection fails.
- The CLIs reach the buckets of their deployments by host name, which compose gives the emulator as aliases: the assets bucket of cdk bootstrap and the deployments bucket of osls.
- The stage prod is synthesized, packaged and validated, never deployed; its account (111111111111), its domain (app.dian-einvoice.example) and the certificate of that domain are placeholders, and its image is named by a placeholder digest (sha256 of zeros) until the delivery pipeline of Infrastructure task 5 pushes it to ECR. The alarms on the dead-letter queues and the estimate of the cost belong to task 5.
- The tests of the AWS adapters and of the handlers of the functions run with the AWS SDK answering from memory and sample events of Lambda; only make cloud-check runs the functions in the emulator, and CI runs it on main only.

## Edge

The edge is the CloudFront distribution in front of the web app and the API (docs/infrastructure.md). The emulator serves the distribution of the stage local and applies its behaviours, the response headers policy of the web app, the custom header of the origin and the origin request policy, and appends the address of the client to X-Forwarded-For, so make cloud-check and make cloud-e2e go through it. What it does not reproduce:

- No AWS WAF: the firewall exists in the stage prod only, checked on the synthesized template (the rules, the overrides that count, the limits and the association); that its rules block what they should and pass the API (bodies of many lines, the certificate, clients without a User-Agent) only a real account would show.
- No cache and no compression: every request reaches the bucket or API Gateway, so the Cache-Control of the files, the TTLs of the cache policy, gzip and Brotli, and an invalidation are checked on the template and on the headers of the files, not on a cache. panel.mjs skips the invalidation in the stage local.
- No CloudFront Functions, even associated with the distribution: the proxy in front of it (cloud-gateway) rewrites the routes of the web app instead, with the same cases as the function, whose code runs in the tests of the CDK app as CloudFront runs it (a plain script with a handler), not in the runtime of CloudFront.
- Its CloudFormation (Floci 2.5.0) keeps of each behaviour only its path, origin, viewer protocol policy, response headers policy and compression, and drops the methods it allows and caches, its cache and origin request policies and its functions; through it the API would get only GET and HEAD, without query strings or cookies. make cloud-up gives the distribution those settings as its deployed template declares them, through the CloudFront API of the emulator (infra/functions/scripts/edge.mjs reconcile): a workaround for the emulator, which creates nothing, changes nothing the template does not declare, refuses to run against anything but the emulator, and is idempotent. It prints, per behaviour, each setting it had to give back; run again with nothing new, it says it keeps what it gave before (the distribution changed after CloudFormation last deployed it); and when a distribution as CloudFormation deployed it already holds every setting, it says that nothing had to be applied and that the step can be retired. make cloud-check checks that the distribution holds its template. AWS applies the template as it is.
- Plain HTTP: the emulator serves the distribution over HTTP whatever its viewer protocol policy says, so the redirect to HTTPS, HTTP/2 and HTTP/3, TLS 1.2 and the certificate of the domain are checked on the template only. HSTS reaches the browser over HTTP on localhost, which ignores it there.
- The emulator replaces the User-Agent of a request with that of CloudFront when the origin request policy does not name it, writes no access logs to the bucket of logs and applies no lifecycle to them, and evaluates no bucket policy: origin access control (only the distribution reads the bucket) is checked on the template.
- The value of the secret of the edge reaches the distribution of the stage local as a parameter of the deployment (the emulator expands dynamic references to Secrets Manager only in the credentials of RDS): make cloud-up pipes it to the CDK CLI, so it is in the parameters of the stack in the emulator (NoEcho hides it from DescribeStacks) and, as in AWS, in the configuration of the distribution, which shows custom headers as they are. Prod resolves it from Secrets Manager. The rotation of the secret is a procedure (docs/infrastructure.md) that make cloud-check does not run.
- The origin verifier runs Node.js 24, the newest runtime of Lambda the emulator has an image of; its first call pulls the image of the Node.js runtime of Lambda (public.ecr.aws/lambda/nodejs). The AWS SDK it uses is the one of that runtime, which AWS updates on its own.

What only a real account would show: that CloudFront replaces an X-Origin-Verify a viewer sends (documented for the custom headers of an origin; the emulator does), the edge locations of South America in the price class, the TLS policy TLSv1.2_2021 with the certificate of ACM, HTTP/3, the managed rules of AWS WAF on real traffic and its limits per address, the authorizer cache of API Gateway (5 minutes per value), the rotation of the secret of the edge while the distribution deploys, the standard logs written with ACLs, and the DNS records of the domain: the alias of the distribution, the validation of the certificate, and the DKIM, MAIL FROM, SPF and DMARC records of SES, which the owner of the domain publishes (no hosted zone is managed here).

## Network and database of prod

The stacks network and database exist in the stage prod only and are never deployed, not even to the emulator; they are proven on what synthesis produces (docs/infrastructure.md). What only a real account could show:

- That AWS takes the templates as written: Aurora MySQL 8.4.8 Serverless v2 with a reader of promotion tier 1, the cluster parameters (require_secure_transport, tls_version, slow_query_log, server_audit_*), the exports to log groups created beforehand, the CA of the instances, the Elastic IPs given to the NAT gateways, the flow logs through their role, the endpoints and their private DNS, and the references between the stacks (Fn::GetStackOutput, the weak references of CDK). The schema validation of CDK and cfn-lint know Aurora MySQL 8.4 up to 8.4.8; 8.4.10 (2026-10-26) is newer than they are.
- The figures taken from the documentation of AWS and not measured: max_connections of 135 at 4 ACU, a failover to the reader typically within 30 seconds and a replacement without one in less than 10 minutes, about 15 s to resume a paused instance, 300 to 1000 MiB of memory for IAM authentication, and the prices (an ACU-hour of Aurora Standard at $0.12, a NAT gateway at $0.045 an hour and per GB, an interface endpoint at about $0.01 an hour per zone and $0.01 per GB, RDS Proxy billed for 8 ACU at least, about $0.015 per ACU-hour as reported for us-east-1, a rate its pricing page did not show when this was written). Task 5 estimates the cost.
- That the rotation functions of AWS run in the private subnets, reach the database and Secrets Manager through the NAT gateway, clone the application user with its privileges on the first rotation and alternate after it, and that the first rotation of the migrations identity, at creation, leaves the cluster usable.
- That the functions verify the certificate of the server against the bundle of RDS, with the name of the cluster endpoint, and that RDS Proxy, when on, takes caching_sha2_password over TLS and the single-user rotation behind it.
- That the security groups and routes behave as the templates say: nothing reaches the database but the functions, RDS Proxy and the rotation functions; the isolated subnets have no way out; a lost NAT gateway affects exactly what docs/infrastructure.md lists; the interface endpoints, when on, exist in the zones chosen (some services offer them in some zones only).
- How the functions ride a failover and a lost zone (the table of failures in docs/infrastructure.md), the reserved concurrency against the quota of the account, and the volume of the audit log and of the flow logs.

## Interface

- Keyboard use, screen readers and contrast were not reviewed by the agent.
- The theme is kept in localStorage and the sidebar state in a cookie; the demo account button exists only on the development server.

## Tests and tooling

- The integration tests run the chain in line (InlineChain) and send email to Laravel's array mailer; the relay, the queue workers and the SMTP leg to the local mailbox are exercised by make checks, make e2e and the stack job of CI.
- Each make e2e and make cloud-e2e run adds an invoice and its payment to Demo Comercial SAS. The tour of make cloud-e2e visits every route and opens the main dialogs without sending them; actions it does not take (sending a form, issuing from the editor) are covered by the journey of the invoice only. The end-to-end test opens the app at http://localhost:8601 inside its container, the origin the API takes for sessions; its files follow the Prettier configuration of the web app, but no linter runs over them.
- The simulator tests use synthetic demo documents as fixtures, with the clock fixed inside the validity of their test certificate.
- Not covered by a test: the 64 KB cap on webhook answers and the limits of the preview and the search.
- PHPStan runs with one worker in the api-analyse container (2 GB): the type shapes of the AWS SDK take about 1.4 GB. vue-tsc checkUnknownProps and checkUnknownEvents stay off (390 attributes and listeners pass through to the shadcn-vue and reka-ui wrappers); the ESLint rules turned off carry their reason in eslint.config.ts. Scramble shows the create rules for the PATCH bodies of taxes and withholdings.
- TypeScript is pinned to 6.x and pnpm runs with a build-script allow list.

## Data and demo

- Every person, company, document and payment of the demo is synthetic. Municipalities come from DIVIPOLA (datos.gov.co), with names title-cased automatically; demo addresses look real.
- The demo history counts back from the day of the run with Carbon test time, while the simulator stamps real dates.
- The DIAN toolbox stays out of git; most of its examples name real companies.
